Syden privacy policy

Last updated October 10, 2026

This is a translation of the original in Portuguese. Syden is run from Brazil, and if the two ever differ, the Portuguese version is the one that applies.

Syden is a free voice, video and chat app. Anyone can create an account by confirming an email address. Joining a community, on the other hand, still takes an invite from someone who is already in it. It is a personal, non-profit project run by Sydenstricker. There are no ads and we don't sell your data — not to advertisers, not to anyone. We use a single measurement tool, and it is described under Site measurement, with what it does and what it doesn't.

What we store

  • Account: your username and your password. The password is never stored as text: we only keep a scrambled version (a hash) that doesn't allow anyone to work it out.
  • Email: anyone creating an account now has to give an address and confirm it before signing in. It is used to give your account back if you forget your password, and to warn you if something serious happens to Syden. Anyone who already had an account before September 27, 2026 was not required to do anything: on those accounts the email remains optional. You can change or remove the address whenever you like, under Settings → My account — if you remove it, a forgotten password can no longer be recovered.
  • Messages: the text of the messages you send, in which channel, and when.
  • Images and sounds you upload: avatar, emojis and soundboard sounds.
  • Badges: which ones you received, when and why, and which ones you chose to show on your profile.
  • Call usage: when you join and leave voice rooms and when you share your screen, for the "Server usage" panel, which only administrators see.
  • Reports: if someone reports a message of yours, we keep a copy of what was reported, the written reason, the name of the person who reported it and yours. The copy exists because the message may be deleted before anyone reviews the report.
  • Moderation log: when someone deletes another person's message, removes a member, deletes an account or grants the administrator role, we record who did it, what they did and when. This is what makes it possible to answer a complaint later — and that is why it keeps the name, not just the account number.
  • Inside each community: the custom roles you were given; level points, if the community turned levels on (counted from messages and time in calls); the warnings and timeouts you received, with the reason, who applied them and when; the reminders you asked Syden for; and the giveaways you won (taking part is just the 🎉 reaction, which is already part of the message).
  • Birthday, if you provide it: only the day and month, never the year. It lets communities that announce birthdays congratulate you on the day. It is optional and can be removed under Settings → My account.
  • Technical logs: the server records the IP address, date and the address requested for each request. They are used to fix problems, are limited to a few megabytes, and the oldest are deleted automatically.

What we don't store

  • Voice, video and shared screens are not recorded. They pass through the server in real time only to reach the other people in the room.
  • We don't collect location, contacts or phone numbers.

Site measurement

The syden.chat address uses Cloudflare Web Analytics, which counts how many visits the page gets and how long it takes to load. It is the project's only measurement tool, and it is here for a practical reason: without measuring, a screen that takes too long to open is not a problem someone fixes — it is a problem nobody finds out about.

It is worth saying what it does not do, because the word "analytics" usually means something worse:

  • It uses no cookies and stores nothing in your browser: no identifier, no local storage.
  • It doesn't recognize you from one visit to the next, and has no way of following you outside Syden.
  • It doesn't read what comes after the "?" in the address. A link with an invite code is not sent.
  • It doesn't fingerprint your device — the technique of identifying someone by the characteristics of their browser.

What it records is the page opened, the load time, the type of browser and the country. This applies to everything served at syden.chat, including the app itself. Voice and video calls don't go through it.

Your preferences, and where each one lives

The app stores a login token on your computer, valid for 30 days. Signing out deletes the token. Changing your password, or using Sign out of the other devices, immediately invalidates the tokens stored on other computers and phones.

Your preferences are split in two, and the rule is: what is yours goes with you; what belongs to the device stays on the device.

  • Stay only on this computer: which microphone, speaker and camera you chose. The names of those devices don't exist on another machine, so taking them along would only cause confusion.
  • Go up to the server, so you don't lose everything when you switch browsers or go from the site to the app: theme, language, each person's volume, notification sounds, visual effects, stream quality, the bunny you chose — and the notes you write about other people.

About the notes, it is worth being direct, because they used to never leave your computer: they are now stored on the server, along with your other preferences. The change has two reasons — you no longer lose them when you switch browsers, and they can be consulted when a report is investigated. They are still yours: nobody else sees them in the app, not even the person you wrote about. When you delete your account, they are deleted with it.

Who sees your data

  • Server members: see your name, avatar, badges, messages, whether you are online and which voice room you are in.
  • Your community's administrator can delete messages, remove members and read the reports made there.
  • Whoever maintains Syden sees only the count for each community: name, how many members, how many messages there were in a period, how many people wrote and when the last one was. It is used to know where Syden is growing and to notice a space being used for something it shouldn't be. The content of conversations doesn't appear in that panel and is not read. Whoever maintains the server technically has access to the whole database — as with any service — and only uses it when there is a report to investigate or a court order.
  • Infrastructure providers: the server is at Hetzner Online GmbH, in Germany (European Union). The site is published through GitHub Pages, which may log visits according to GitHub's policy. Confirmation and password-recovery emails are sent through Resend, from the São Paulo region — only your address and the text of the message pass through it. The server's address uses the DuckDNS service only for name resolution on the internet.
  • Cloudflare: the pages of syden.chat pass through it before reaching you. It delivers the site, protects sign-up against bots (Turnstile) and performs the measurement described above. Because it sits on that path, it sees the IP address and the page requested on each visit — this is true even with measurement turned off, and it is the price of having the site protected by it. Voice and video calls don't go through Cloudflare.
  • GIPHY — only when there is a GIF in the conversation. The GIF picker searches GIPHY's library, and the images are loaded from their servers: Syden keeps no copy. This means that when you open a conversation that has a GIF, your browser makes a request to GIPHY, and GIPHY sees your IP address and the address of the image — as happens with any image hosted somewhere else.

    What you type in the GIF search goes to them, because that is the search; it goes through our server, without your name or your account attached. None of this happens if you don't open the picker and there is no GIF in the conversation.
  • Google Fonts — only if Syden is in a language with a different script. Anyone using Syden in Arabic, Hebrew, Hindi, Bengali, Tamil, Telugu, Thai, Burmese, Amharic, Chinese, Japanese, Korean or another script that is not Latin, Cyrillic or Greek needs a font that draws those letters, and it is downloaded from Google Fonts. At that moment Google sees your IP address and the name of the font requested — nothing from your account or your conversations. In Portuguese, English, Spanish, Russian, Greek and the other languages of those three scripts, Syden uses the letters already on your device and doesn't talk to Google.

    The fonts a community chooses for its name, and the font of your name on your profile, are stored in Syden itself and don't come from outside.
  • Project Arachnid (Canada) — every image you upload. Before an image is stored — avatar, community image, emoji or message attachment — it is checked against a database of known child sexual abuse material, maintained by the Canadian Centre for Child Protection. The image is sent to them for that check, and that is why this must be said plainly instead of being hidden in a technical line.

    Each image is checked only once. The result is stored by the file's code, so uploading the same photo again doesn't send it again. Audio, video and documents don't go there — the database is of images. And the content of your text conversations is never sent to anyone.

    This exists because a server that hosts people's images must block that material, and because Brazilian law (ECA Digital) and German law — where the server is — require removal and reporting to the authorities. When an image is rejected, a record of what happened is kept; the image is never stored.
  • Google, Discord, GitHub or Steam — only if you choose to sign in with them. At that moment the provider learns that you signed in to Syden, and Syden receives from it your name, your photo and your email when there is one (Steam doesn't provide email). We ask for nothing beyond that: we don't read your messages, we don't see your contacts and we don't post anything on your behalf. Unlinking it under Settings → My account → Sign in with ends the matter.

We don't share your data with anyone else, except when required by law.

For how long

For as long as your account exists. When you delete your account, we immediately delete your messages, your avatar, your email, your badges and your usage history — with a 90-day exception, explained right below. Server backups may keep this data for up to 7 days before being replaced. Channels, emojis and sounds you created remain available to the group, without your name.

Two exceptions, to be clear: the moderation log and reports remain stored, with the name written, even after the account is deleted. Without this there would be no way to answer, months later, why a message was deleted or an account removed. They are not used for any other purpose.

The 90 days after deleting your account

This is the part you need to know before you press Delete account, and that is why it has a heading of its own. The account disappears from Syden immediately — nobody can find it anymore, your messages vanish from conversations, your name leaves the lists. But a copy of what you wrote is kept for 90 days, outside the app, and only then deleted for good.

Why this exists. A court request usually arrives weeks after whatever prompted it. Without these 90 days, someone who committed a crime on Syden could delete their account and take the evidence with them — and we would have nothing to hand over to an authority that asks. It is also what Brazilian law expects from anyone hosting other people's conversations.

What is kept: who you were (username, email, when you joined), what you wrote, when, in which channel, and the files you attached. What isn't kept: your password — not even scrambled. It is not evidence of anything, so there is no reason to keep it.

Nobody reads this through the app. There is no screen, button or address that shows this content — not even to whoever administers Syden. It can only be read by someone with direct access to the server, with a command made for that purpose, and that access is logged on the machine itself. After the 90 days, the server deletes it on its own, without depending on anyone remembering.

Your rights

Under Brazil's General Data Protection Law (LGPD), you can request access to, correction of or deletion of your data. Two things you can do yourself in the app, without asking anyone:

  • Take your data: download a file with everything Syden stores about you.
  • Delete your account: under Settings → My account → Delete account. The account disappears from Syden immediately; a copy of what you wrote stays outside the app for 90 days, in case an authority requests it — see The 90 days after deleting your account, above.

For other requests, talk to your server's administrator or open a request at github.com/Sydenstricker/Syden/issues.

Security

All communication with the server is encrypted (HTTPS and secure media connections). The server has a firewall and only accepts the connections it needs, and it limits how many password attempts anyone can make, so that nobody can keep guessing yours. No system is 100% secure, but we take care to reduce the risks.

Age

Syden is intended for people over 13. Minors need permission from their parents or guardians.
